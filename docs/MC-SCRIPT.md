# The MC script

Words to say out loud, in order, on a trivia night. Written to be read off a
phone or a printed page while standing in front of the room.

**Spoken lines are in plain text. Anything in _italics_ is a stage direction,
not something you say.** Square brackets are blanks you fill in: `[venue]`,
`[room code]`.

This is the script for the night. The shape of the game and its numbers live in
`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`. If a number here ever disagrees with the Setup panel on
your phone, the panel is right and this file is stale.

The equipment and the setup live in `docs/HOSTING.md`, which still describes the
old setup where the game ran off a laptop on the room's own wifi. It has not
been rewritten for running from the server, so take its hardware advice with
that in mind: tonight the room needs working internet, and the wifi warnings in
there no longer apply.

---

## Before you pick up the microphone

_Five things, all on the big screen or your phone. Two minutes._

1. The big screen is on `/host` and shows the venue name, the QR code and the
   room code. The room code is also in the top-right pill all night.
2. **Click the sound button on the big screen.** You will hear three notes.
   That chord is the whole sound check: it means the browser let the audio
   through and the room can hear it. No chord, no sound all night, so fix it
   now. Nothing else makes a sound until the first question.
3. Open `/control` on your phone. Check the Sound panel says `on`, and check
   Questions says a number of questions in play that is bigger than the night
   needs.
4. Walk to the back of the room and read the room code off the screen. If you
   cannot read it from the furthest table, nobody at that table can join.
5. Put a tablet on every table that asked for one. _See "Groups" below._

## 1. Welcome

Good evening, everybody, and welcome to trivia at [venue].

Here is how tonight works. We play three games. Each game is two rounds of ten
questions, and then a final wager to finish it. There is a short break between
the rounds and a longer one between the games, so you will get to the bar.

Everything happens on the big screen and on your phone. You do not need a pen,
you do not need paper, and you do not need to shout anything out.

## 2. Getting in

Get your phones out now, because this is the part that takes the longest and I
would rather we did it together than one table at a time.

Look at the big screen. There are two steps.

_Point at the screen. Do not rush this; say each step twice._

**One.** Point your camera at the square code and tap the link that comes up.
If your camera will not do it, type the address underneath it into any web
browser. That is all it is, a web page. There is nothing to download and no app
to install.

**Two.** It will ask you for two things: a name, and the room code. The room
code is the big one on the screen, and it is **[room code]**. Say it with me:
[room code].

_Wait. Watch the "playing" count on the big screen climb. Do not move on until
it stops climbing._

Your name is how you will appear on the scoreboard all night, so pick something
you want read out when you win. One rule: no two teams can have the same name,
so if it tells you the name is taken, somebody beat you to it.

_If a table is stuck:_ the two most common problems are a typo in the room code
and being on a different wifi network. Have them read the code back to you off
the screen.

## 3. Groups

If you are playing as a group, **share one device**. One phone, or one of the
tablets on the table. Pick whoever has the best eyes and put it in the middle.

The game scores a team, not a phone, so a group on four separate phones is four
separate teams fighting each other, and the four of you will split your own
points four ways. One device per team.

_This is the single most common mistake. Say it even if you think it is obvious,
and say it again when you see a table with four phones out._

## 4. How the scoring works

This is the only unusual thing about tonight, so I will go through it slowly.

Every question is worth up to a thousand points. Not two hundred, not twenty. A
thousand.

But the value falls while the question is up. You get twenty seconds, and you
can see the number falling on your own screen the whole time.

- The **first five seconds are free.** Read the question, read all four
  answers, take your time. It is worth the full thousand that whole time.
- After that it slides. Around the ten second mark it is worth about seven
  hundred and thirty. At fifteen seconds, about four hundred and seventy.
- At the buzzer it is still worth **two hundred.** It never goes to zero. A
  right answer late is still a right answer and it still pays.

So two teams who both knew it are separated by how fast they were sure. That is
the game.

A wrong answer costs you nothing. Answer every single question, even if you are
guessing between two. There is no penalty for being wrong, so there is never a
reason to sit one out.

## 5. Two more things about answering

**You can change your mind.** Tap a different letter any time before the lock
and the new one counts. But the clock does not forgive you: your answer is
priced from the moment you landed on the letter you finish with. Your phone
tells you what you are holding and what a fresh tap is worth right now, so you
can see what changing your mind costs. Tapping the same letter again costs you
nothing, so tap away if it makes you feel better.

**The question stays up the full twenty seconds**, even when every table has
already answered. Nobody can rush you by being fast. Use the time.

## 6. The wager

Each game ends with one final question, and before you see it you place a bet.

You can bet anything from nothing at all up to your whole score. If you have
less than a thousand on the board you can still bet up to a thousand, so nobody
is ever locked out of the last question.

Get it right and you win what you bet. Get it wrong and you lose what you bet.
Bet nothing and nothing happens to you, which is sometimes the right play.

On this one question only, **speed does not matter.** The clock is not taking
points off you. It is your bet, win or lose, however fast you answer.

You get forty seconds to place the bet. **If you do not place a bet, you have
bet nothing** — it does not bet for you.

And once you have bet, **you must answer.** If you place a bet and then do not
put an answer in before the lock, you lose the bet. The bet is the commitment.
So if you have money on it, put something in, even a guess.

_Say that last one every single time. It is the rule that costs somebody a game
and it is the one nobody expects._

_Say this at the first wager of the night, not during the welcome. In the
welcome just say "and a final wager closes each game."_

## 7. Starting

Right. Is everybody in? Last call for the room code: **[room code]**.

_Check the "playing" count against the room. Then start the game from your
phone._

Here we go. Game one, round one.

## Through the night

_Short lines for the moments that come round again. You do not need a script
for most of the night, but these are the four that go wrong without one._

**Before the first question of a round.** The screen counts you in and says
"get ready, set, go" out loud. Let it do that. Do not talk over the count-in,
because the question appears on the word "go" and the five free seconds start
there.

**On the reveal.** Read the answer out, then read the fact underneath it. The
music is off during the reveal and the next question does not come for ten
seconds, so this is your window. This is where you host.

**Into a break.** That is round one. Five minutes. The clock is on the screen
and on your phones, and we start again by ourselves when it hits zero, so do
not go far.

_The break ends itself. If you need it to wait for you, turn off
"start automatically after a break" in the Setup panel first._

**Into the long break.** That is game one. Here are the standings. Ten minutes,
get a drink, and we will do it all again.

**End of the night.** That is the night. Your winners, with [score] points:
[team]. Thank you all for coming, and we will see you next time.

**Starting a second night on the same evening.** Resetting the night gives you
a brand new room code, and everybody has to join again under it. Nobody needs
to reload anything: every phone still open drops back to the join form by
itself. Say "new code on the screen, it is **[room code]**, join again" and
read it out twice. Their names are still saved on their own phones, so most
people only have to type the code.

## When something goes wrong

_What to say, so you are not improvising in front of a room._

**A table cannot get in.** "Give me ten seconds." Check two things: the room
code they typed, and that they are on the right wifi. Do not debug it from the
stage; start the game and fix them during the first break. A team that joins
late keeps every point from the moment they join.

**A phone dropped out mid-question.** "Reload the page, you will come straight
back." They land back on their own team with their own score. Nothing is lost
and nobody needs to re-join or re-enter the room code.

**Somebody says their score is wrong.** It is almost always the falling clock:
they answered at seventeen seconds and expected a thousand. "You had it right,
you had it late. Look at the number on your screen next time, it is counting
down." Check it in the Teams panel during the break if they push.

**The room cannot hear you over the music.** Music only plays under a live
question now, never while you are talking. If it is still in your way, the
volume slider is in the Sound panel, and the Big-screen sound toggle kills it
outright. You do not have to stop the game to do either.

**The room has gone quiet and bored.** You are between questions for ten
seconds and in intros for eight. Those are yours. Read the fun fact, pick on
the table in the lead, ask the room if anybody knew that one.
